SCC is a research program investigating whether safety-relevant or otherwise protected model behavior can be made *structurally load-bearing* for general model capability.

The core target is not ordinary behavioral alignment and not ordinary tamper resistance. SCC asks whether training can shape a model such that selectively removing a protected property while preserving general capability becomes difficult, expensive, or impossible within a defined modification budget.

The intended failure mode is **fail-closed**:

> If a sufficiently strong attacker succeeds in degrading the protected property, the same intervention should cause disproportionate degradation of indispensable model capabilities.

This repository describes the initial SCC program, experimental vocabulary, scaling ladder, training objectives, attack suite, evaluation methodology, milestones, related work, and open questions.

## Core hypothesis

Let:

- `C(theta)` = general model capability
- `S(theta)` = protected safety property or synthetic invariant
- `theta'` = a modified model derived from checkpoint `theta`

SCC seeks models for which, over a defined attack class:

`S(theta') < S_min  =>  C(theta') < C_min`

Equivalently, the high-capability / low-safety region should be hard to reach from the released checkpoint.

The program therefore studies the **escape cost**:

`E(theta) = minimum modification cost required to obtain high capability while degrading the protected property.`

The long-term objective is to increase `E(theta)` substantially, ideally until recovery of high capability after safety removal approaches the cost of rebuilding the model rather than editing it.

## What SCC is not

SCC does **not** claim to solve alignment. In particular, it does not by itself solve:

- a dangerous policy already encoded in an intact model;
- inference-time circumvention that does not require modifying protected weights;
- governance of datacenters or creation of successor systems;
- specification of the correct human values to protect.

Those are distinct layers of the safety stack. SCC isolates the **self-/weight-modification channel**.

## Initial experimental philosophy

The first experiments should use a **synthetic protected invariant**, not real harmful-content refusal. This keeps the target objective measurable and avoids confounding safety judgments with the mechanism itself.

The attacker is external and maximally privileged: white-box access, gradients, training data, and knowledge of the defense. The model does not need AGI-like agency for the primitive to be tested.

## Scaling ladder

1. **Mechanism scale: 100–400M parameters** — determine whether intentional coupling can be created at all.
2. **Serious test scale: ~0.7–1.5B** — test robustness against broad modification attacks and establish repeatability.
3. **Scaling scale: ~2.5–4B** — determine whether representational redundancy defeats or strengthens coupling.
4. **Capstone scale: ~6–8B** — validate that the phenomenon survives in a meaningfully capable sub-10B model.

Start small. Scaling is earned by positive evidence, not assumed.

## Working terminology

**SCC** — Safety-Capability Coupling, the overall research program.

**IC coupling** — Invariant-Capability Coupling, the synthetic experimental primitive used before making genuine safety claims.

**Protected invariant** — the behavior/property intentionally coupled to model capability.

**Escape** — a modified checkpoint with low protected-property performance but preserved general capability.

**Escape cost** — resources required to produce an escape.

**Forbidden quadrant** — high capability + low protected-property performance.

**Capability collapse** — desired degradation of general capability after successful removal of the protected property.

**Fail-closed model** — a model for which successful safety removal causes loss of the capability the attacker intended to preserve.
